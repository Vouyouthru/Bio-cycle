01 • THE WASTE STREAM

Organic matter is still a resource.

Food scraps, animal manure and agricultural residues contain biodegradable material. When these resources are not recovered, their potential value is lost from the productive cycle.

🐄

Animal manure

Livestock operations continuously produce organic residues that require appropriate collection, handling and treatment.

🍎

Food waste

Food processing, markets, restaurants and households can generate biodegradable material every day.

🌾

Agricultural residues

Crop residues and other biomass can form additional organic streams that may be suitable for resource recovery.

```
```

02 • WHEN MANAGEMENT IS INEFFICIENT

A resource can become a problem.

The consequences depend on the type of waste, local management practices and environmental conditions. Several challenges can emerge when organic material is poorly managed.

💨

Emissions

Decomposition under uncontrolled conditions can release gases into the atmosphere. Capturing biogas provides one pathway for controlled resource recovery.

💧

Water concerns

Poorly managed organic residues can contribute to contamination risks when liquids and waste enter surrounding environments.

🦟

Local sanitation

Accumulated organic waste can create unpleasant odors and conditions that attract insects and other vectors.

```
```

03 • THE HIDDEN VALUE

What disappears when waste is discarded?

Organic material contains several forms of value. A circular system attempts to recover those values rather than treating the material as having no further use.

⚡

Energy

Biodegradable material can contain chemical energy that microorganisms can convert into methane-rich biogas.

🧪

Organic matter

Anaerobic digestion does not simply eliminate material. It transforms it into biogas and a remaining digestate stream.

🌱

Nutrients

Depending on treatment and application, digestate can retain nutrients and organic matter that may support agricultural use.

```
```

04 • TWO DIFFERENT PATHWAYS

Linear thinking vs circular thinking.

The difference is not simply where waste goes. It is whether materials are treated as an endpoint or as inputs for another process.

➡

Linear pathway

Resource → use → waste → disposal

Valuable material and energy can leave the productive system after a single use.

🔄

Circular pathway

Resource → use → recovery → new resource

Materials are redirected toward another useful application wherever practical.

♻

BioCycle approach

Organic waste → digestion → biogas + digestate → useful applications

The objective is to demonstrate how a biological waste stream can participate in a resource cycle.

05 • THE OPPORTUNITY

Why consider biogas?

Anaerobic digestion provides a biological pathway for treating suitable organic materials while producing a combustible biogas stream.

🦠

Biological conversion

Naturally occurring microorganisms perform the conversion inside an oxygen-free environment.

🔥

Gas production

The digestion process produces biogas, containing methane along with carbon dioxide and other gases.

🌱

Resource recovery

Alongside biogas, the process produces digestate that can potentially be managed for beneficial use.
